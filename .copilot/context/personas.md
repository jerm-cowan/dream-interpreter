# User Personas — Dream Reflection

Two personas, representing the two most common motivations for opening this app. Both are the same "core user" described in the brief, just entering with slightly different energy — the product should serve both without forcing them into different modes.

---

## Persona 1: "Maya, the Morning Processor"

**Age/context:** 29, marketing coordinator, phone always within reach.

**Scenario:** Maya wakes up from a vivid, slightly unsettling dream — she was back in her childhood home, but the rooms kept rearranging. She can't shake the feeling of it and reaches for her phone before she's even out of bed.

**Goals:**
- Make sense of a feeling, not just a plot. She wants to know *why it felt like that*, not just "what houses symbolize."
- Get something in under two minutes — she has to get up for work soon.
- Feel like the app "gets" that this was emotionally significant, without being melodramatic about it.

**Frustrations with existing tools:**
- Generic "dream dictionaries" that give a single flat definition ("houses = the self") with no nuance.
- Chatbots that require her to keep prompting and steering to get anything useful.

**What good looks like for Maya:** She types two or three sentences, taps generate, and within seconds has something that feels like it was actually written *about her dream* — not a templated answer. She leaves the slider near the Reflective end, and the app's calmer visual theme at that setting reinforces the mood she's in.

---

## Persona 2: "Theo, the Curious Skeptic"

**Age/context:** 34, software engineer, enjoys pop psychology and folklore but doesn't believe dreams "mean" anything literal.

**Scenario:** Theo's partner mentioned a bizarre dream at breakfast (being chased by a talking vending machine) and joked "what do you think that means?" Theo pulls out Dream Reflection half as a joke, half genuinely curious what different frameworks would say.

**Goals:**
- Compare the "serious" science-adjacent explanation with the folklore/symbolism take, mostly for fun and conversation.
- See where different schools of thought actually *agree*, which he finds more interesting than any single explanation.
- Not be talked down to or told the dream "means" something definite — he'll bounce immediately if the tone feels new-age-certain.

**Frustrations with existing tools:**
- Tools that present symbolism as fact ("seeing a vending machine means...") with zero acknowledgment of uncertainty.
- No neuroscience-flavored option anywhere — everything is either astrology-coded or nothing.

**What good looks like for Theo:** He drags the tone slider toward Playful and is delighted that the whole app visibly brightens and loosens up as he does it — not just the copy, but the actual look of the page. He reads all three cards partly for entertainment, but is pleasantly surprised the Neuroscience lens holds up as genuinely informative. The Divergent Interpretations section is his favorite part — it's where the app earns his trust by not pretending the lenses agree on everything.

---

## Why two personas, one product

Maya and Theo represent the emotional-processing end and the curious-comparison end of the same core value proposition. Neither persona should require a different mode or path through the app — the tone slider (and the visual theme shift it drives) and the structure of the three lenses are what let a single, simple product flex to serve both.
